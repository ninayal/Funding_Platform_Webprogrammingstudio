MissionJuly 6, 2026

Our Developer Mission: Building Digital Bridges to Living Craft

A platform can flatten culture into inventory, or it can preserve context. We are choosing the second path, one product decision at a time.

LC
Làng & Co. Development TeamProduct · Engineering · Research

Làng & Co. began with a simple frustration: beautifully made objects were often presented online without the knowledge that made them meaningful. A vase became a decorative SKU. A woven surface became a trend. A village name became a marketing adjective.

We are building for context, not just conversion

Our development mission is to make provenance part of the product architecture. That means the maker, place, material, process, and uncertainty around a claim are treated as first-class information rather than optional copy at the bottom of a page.

Technology should help a craft object carry more of its story forward, not strip that story away for the sake of a cleaner card layout.

In practical terms, we are designing structured fields for maker identity, craft village, material, process notes, care guidance, and source references. We are also resisting the temptation to fill empty fields with confident assumptions. When we do not know, the platform should be able to say so.

A marketplace is also an editorial system

The journal, forum, wishlist, and shop are not separate ideas. Together they form a context system. A customer may first encounter a technique through a story, ask a question in the forum, save an object for later, and only then decide to buy. That slower path is intentional.

We measure success not only by completed orders, but by whether people leave with a clearer understanding of how an object was made and why its differences matter.

What comes next

Our next technical priorities are stronger provenance records, accessible maker profiles, safer moderation tools, and better ways for artisans to update their own information without losing editorial support. The mission is not to digitise craft for its own sake. It is to build infrastructure that helps living craft remain visible, legible, and economically viable.

GuideJune 28, 2026

Bát Tràng Beyond Blue and White

A village is not a style preset. It is a living network of kilns, families, experiments, failures, and changing markets.

MA
Mai AnField editor

Bát Tràng is often reduced to a familiar visual shorthand. But the village contains many different workshops, firing practices, family histories, and contemporary directions.

Walking from one kiln to another reveals a much wider vocabulary: ash glazes, carved surfaces, revived forms, experimental firing, commissioned tableware, and objects made for everyday domestic use.

Looking beyond the motif

To understand a piece, ask not only what it depicts, but who made it, which body of clay was used, how the glaze behaves, and what kind of firing produced the surface.

GuideMay 30, 2026

How to Live with Handmade Ceramics

Care is less about perfection than understanding material behaviour.

TH
Thu HàCare editor

Start with the maker's guidance whenever it is available. Different clay bodies, glazes, decorations, and firing temperatures behave differently.

Avoid sudden thermal shock, store pieces where rims are not forced against hard edges, and do not assume every handmade surface is dishwasher-safe.

What a Craft Community Needs from a Platform

Visibility is useful only when it arrives with context, control, and practical value.

HB
Huy BaCommunity contributor

In conversations with makers, the same concerns return: pricing pressure, loss of context, copied images, and the burden of maintaining another digital profile.

A useful platform should reduce those burdens rather than move them around. That means clear ownership controls, realistic moderation, transparent fees, and editorial support that does not erase the maker's voice.
